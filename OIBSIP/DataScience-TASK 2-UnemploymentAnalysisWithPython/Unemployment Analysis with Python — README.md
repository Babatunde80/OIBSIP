# Unemployment Analysis with Python

## 1. Problem

The objective of this project is to analyze **unemployment trends across India** and examine how unemployment, employment, and labour-force participation changed during the early **COVID-19 period**.

## 2. Methodology

The project followed a structured exploratory data analysis workflow:

- **Data Cleaning:** Inspected the dataset for missing values, duplicates, data types, and categorical variables before removing empty observations.
- **Regional Analysis:** Calculated average unemployment rates across Indian states and regions to identify areas with the highest unemployment.
- **Time-Series Analysis:** Examined monthly unemployment trends and compared patterns across selected states.
- **Correlation Analysis:** Evaluated relationships between unemployment, estimated employment, and labour participation.
- **COVID-19 Analysis:** Compared labour-market indicators before and during the COVID-19 period to quantify changes in unemployment and employment conditions.
- **Data Visualization:** Used bar charts, line charts, and a correlation heatmap to communicate key findings.

## 3. Skills

**Python | Pandas | Matplotlib | Seaborn | Data Cleaning | Exploratory Data Analysis | Data Visualization | Time-Series Analysis | Correlation Analysis | Labour-Market Analysis**

## 4. Results

The analysis identified a substantial deterioration in labour-market conditions during the early COVID-19 period:

- Average unemployment increased from **9.51% pre-COVID to 17.77% during the COVID-19 period**, an increase of **8.26 percentage points (86.91%)**.
- Average estimated employment declined from approximately **7.47 million to 6.52 million**.
- Labour participation declined from **43.89% to 39.33%**.
- **Tripura (28.35%)** and **Haryana (26.28%)** recorded the highest average unemployment rates among the regions analyzed.
- Monthly unemployment rose sharply in **April 2020 (23.64%)** and **May 2020 (24.88%)**.

## 5. Conclusion

The analysis indicates that the early **COVID-19 period coincided with a substantial negative shock to India's labour market**. Higher unemployment was accompanied by lower estimated employment and reduced labour-force participation, with April and May 2020 showing the most pronounced disruption.
